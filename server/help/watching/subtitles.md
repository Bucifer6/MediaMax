## Turning subtitles on

In the player, open the **Subtitles** menu and choose a track, or press <kbd>C</kbd> to step through them. On a TV remote the **Red** button does the same.

To choose automatically every time, set **Settings → Playback**:

- **Preferred subtitle language**.
- **Subtitles**: *Off*, *Auto (when audio is foreign)*, *Always on*, or *Forced only* (only the lines meant for everyone, such as signs or foreign dialogue).

## Changing how they look

**Settings → Playback → Subtitles** shows a live preview. You can change:

- **Size**, **Colour**, **Background** (none, drop shadow, translucent or solid box) and **Font**.
- **Text edge**: outline, drop shadow or none.
- **Position** and **Lift from bottom**, to move them above text burnt into the picture.
- **ASS/SSA styling**: *Auto* keeps the file's own fancy styles (karaoke, signs, colours) where your device can draw them; *My style* shows plain text in your style.

The same options are in the player under **Subtitles → Subtitle style…**.

## When subtitles are early or late

Open **Subtitles → Sync & delay** in the player, or press <kbd>G</kbd> (earlier) and <kbd>H</kbd> (later) in 0.1-second steps. The change applies to this video.

## Finding missing subtitles

If your server has OpenSubtitles set up, **Subtitles → Search online…** in the player finds and downloads subtitles in your language.

## Picture-based subtitles

Blu-ray (PGS) subtitles are drawn by the player. DVD and some broadcast subtitles have to be drawn into the picture by the server ("burned in"), which needs a conversion and may take a moment to start.
