## Auto (the default)

With **Auto**, the player measures your connection when a video starts and keeps measuring while you watch:

- If the original file fits your connection, it plays as it is (**direct play**), in full quality.
- If not, the server converts it to a quality that fits.
- When the connection gets worse, quality drops quickly so the video doesn't stop. A message says so, for example *"Lowered quality to 720p due to connection speed"*.
- When the connection improves, quality goes back up one step at a time, and back to the original file when it fits again.

## Choosing a fixed quality

- **For one video**: open the quality menu in the player and pick a level, such as *Original* or *1080p · 8 Mbps*.
- **As your default**: **Settings → Playback → Streaming quality**.

A fixed choice turns Auto off. *Original* always sends the file as it is, even on a slow connection.

| Quality | Data per hour (about) |
|---|---|
| 4K · 40 Mbps | 18 GB |
| 1080p · 8 Mbps | 3.6 GB |
| 720p · 4 Mbps | 1.8 GB |
| 480p · 1.5 Mbps | 0.7 GB |
| 360p · 720 kbps | 0.3 GB |

## Saving mobile data

Pick **720p · 3 Mbps** or lower as your default on your phone, or download titles on Wi-Fi to watch later (see [Downloads and offline viewing](help:downloads)).

## Why isn't it in full quality?

Your admin may have set a limit for streams outside the home network, or your account may not be allowed to use conversion. Press <kbd>I</kbd> in the player: the *Quality* line shows the current level and the measured bandwidth.
