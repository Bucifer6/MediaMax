## Choosing an audio track

Open the **Audio** menu in the player, or press <kbd>A</kbd> to switch to the next track (the **Green** button on a TV remote). To pick a language automatically, set **Settings → Playback → Preferred audio language**.

Badges such as **Dolby Atmos**, **DTS:X**, **TrueHD** and **5.1** show what each track contains.

## Listening comforts

Turn these on in **Settings → Playback → Audio** (they apply on all your devices), or from the player's audio menu:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Night mode** | Quiet dialogue gets louder and explosions softer, so you don't have to keep changing the volume. |
| **Dialogue boost** | Lifts the voices above music and effects. |
| **Normalise volume** | Evens out loudness between titles. |

On TVs and phones, and for *Normalise volume*, the server re-encodes the sound to apply these. The picture is untouched, but Dolby Atmos or DTS:X sound becomes ordinary surround while the option is on.

## Sound out of step with the picture (lip-sync)

If a soundbar or AV receiver makes the sound lag behind the lips, set **Settings → Playback → Audio → Lip-sync on this device**. Positive values delay the sound; negative values make it earlier. This is saved per device, so the TV and the laptop can differ.

For a single video, use **Sync & delay** in the player instead.

## Surround sound and receivers

{{product}} sends surround sound as it is when your device or receiver can play it, and converts it otherwise. Browsers often report only stereo even with a receiver connected, so you may get stereo in a browser and surround in the TV app. **Settings → Playback → Audio** shows what this device reported.

No sound at all? See [No sound or the wrong audio](help:no-sound).
